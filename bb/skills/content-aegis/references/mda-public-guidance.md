# MDA public guidance and programme information

Use this reference for evergreen public information produced from MDA material when the main user need is to understand, prepare, take part or find the right route.

Do not use it for:

- an office profile or role-holder page
- a transactional start page or form
- internal routing instructions
- news, speeches, press releases, campaigns, political communications or social media

## Core rule

Start with what the public needs to know or do. Do not reproduce the MDA document, its organisational structure or the order in which it supplied information.

The MDA supplies service, policy and operational facts. GovTech controls content structure, plain language, accessibility and user-centred presentation.

## Decide whether this is the right model

Use public guidance when the main need is to:

- understand a rule, process or responsibility
- prepare before taking an action elsewhere
- understand a programme and how to take part
- find a location or contact route for a defined purpose
- compare routes before choosing one

Use a service or start page when the main need is to begin a transaction.

Use a routing page when users need to choose between distinct destinations.

Use an office page only when the office itself is a useful destination and users need to decide whether to contact it.

If one source contains several needs, recommend separate content rather than one long MDA page.

## Establish the user need

Before drafting, identify:

- who needs the information
- what they are trying to understand or do
- when they need it
- what decision or next action the page should support

If this cannot be determined, provide structure only and ask the service owner. Do not use “raise awareness” as a sufficient user need.

## Transform MDA source material

Extract supported facts, then reorganise them around the user task.

Remove unless essential to the task:

- institutional history
- mission and vision statements
- organisational charts
- executive messages
- praise, achievements and reputational claims
- internal process language
- unexplained programme or policy language
- repeated descriptions of the MDA

Preserve legal and policy meaning. Explain unfamiliar formal terms in plain English once when doing so does not alter the meaning.

Do not convert policy uncertainty into confident public guidance.

## Structure guidance around the task

Choose headings that answer likely user questions. A useful sequence may include:

```markdown
# [Task-based title]

[One or 2 sentence summary that states who the information is for and what it helps them do.]

## Who this applies to

## What you need to know

## What you need to do

## When you need to do it

## What happens next

## Get help with this
```

Use only sections supported by the need and evidence. Do not force this sequence onto every page.

Titles should use the words users recognise and describe the task or subject. Do not lead with an internal programme name unless users know and search for it.

## Programme information

Programme content must help someone decide whether and how to take part.

Include only when confirmed:

- what the programme provides
- who it is for
- eligibility or selection rules
- opening and closing dates
- cost or financial support
- locations or delivery channels
- what someone must provide
- how to register, apply or take part
- what happens after they act
- where to get help for a defined purpose

Do not describe a programme as important, exciting, transformative, successful or beneficial without a user need and evidence. Replace promotional claims with specific facts.

If an application or registration exists, link to the correct service or start page. Do not duplicate the complete transaction on the guidance page.

## Policy and rules

State what the rule means for the affected user and what they must do.

Check:

- who the rule applies to
- exceptions
- dates and transition arrangements
- consequences that can safely be stated
- the action or route available to the user

Do not infer these from a policy title or general purpose statement.

Quote legislation only when exact wording is necessary. Otherwise explain the effect accurately and retain a source reference in review notes.

Escalate legal interpretation to legal advisers and policy decisions to the policy owner.

## Contact information

Include contact information only when it helps complete the page’s user task.

For every route, state its purpose. For example:

`Email [address] if you need help with [specific issue].`

Do not:

- publish a directory without a user need
- mix internal and public contacts
- use personal staff details when a maintained role-based route exists
- promise a response time without confirmation
- copy contact reasons from an office description

When several routes exist, organise them by user need rather than channel or team structure.

## Locations and opening information

Include locations only when users must visit, collect, submit, attend or get in-person help.

For each location, confirm as applicable:

- full public name
- complete address
- purpose of the location
- opening days and hours
- appointment requirement
- accessible route or contact for accessibility support
- temporary or seasonal limits

Do not infer opening hours or availability. Do not use a map or landmark as the only way to understand an address.

If many locations share the same rules, state the common rules once and keep each location entry scannable.

## Dates and time-sensitive content

For application windows, events, temporary arrangements or scheduled changes:

- use exact dates
- include the year
- include a time and time zone when the deadline depends on it
- state what closes or changes
- identify what users can do afterwards, if confirmed

Do not describe time-limited information as evergreen.

Recommend a start and removal or review date for temporary content.

## Maintenance and freshness

Before treating guidance as ready for factual confirmation, record:

- the factual owner
- the content maintenance owner or route
- the source and date checked for consequential facts
- known review triggers
- a review date when facts can change without a clear event

Use event-based triggers where possible, including:

- policy or legislation changes
- programme opening or closing
- fee or eligibility changes
- location, contact or opening-hour changes
- channel or process changes

If no owner can maintain changeable content, show this as a publishing risk.

## Relationship to transactions

When guidance leads to a transaction:

- make the next action explicit
- link to the correct entry or start page
- keep eligibility, documents, fees, deadlines and terminology consistent
- do not place the form link before information users must know
- run journey-parity QA

Do not duplicate full preparation guidance on both pages unless the repetition is necessary at the point of action.

## Public-guidance QA

### Need and model

- one primary audience and need are clear
- the model supports the task
- separate needs have not been forced into one page
- a transaction, routing page or office page is used where more appropriate

### Evidence

- consequential facts are supported
- policy or legal uncertainty remains visible
- no internal information is exposed
- dates, contacts and locations have current sources

### Content

- title and headings use public language
- summary supports a decision or action
- institutional background and promotion are removed
- programme claims are factual
- unfamiliar terms are explained without changing meaning
- the next action is clear

### Contact and location

- each contact has a purpose
- every location is tied to a user need
- addresses and hours are usable without layout context
- no unconfirmed response expectation appears

### Maintenance

- factual and maintenance ownership are known
- changeable information has triggers or a review date
- temporary information has a removal or review plan

### Journey

- related service content uses the same facts and terms
- links lead to the correct stage
- essential preparation appears before the transaction
