# Use the GovTech content style guide

Use this guide when writing or reviewing public-facing GovTech Barbados content. It covers our agreed approach to punctuation, capitalisation, dates, times, numbers, money and abbreviations.

Use it after checking that the content is in plain English and before completing the accessibility and mobile checks.

The guide does not replace our guidance on evidence, service structure, accessibility or specialist patterns.

## How to use this guide

Follow these rules unless you need to preserve:

- exact legal wording
- a prescribed statement
- an official name
- wording that has an agreed service or policy meaning

Do not repeat inconsistent punctuation or capitalisation simply because it appears in an existing page, document or paper form.

If changing the content could affect its legal, policy or service meaning, check with the appropriate owner first.

## Use UK English

Use UK English spelling and grammar.

For example:

- licence as the noun and license as the verb
- organise, not organize
- centre, not center
- fill in a form, not fill out a form

Keep the original spelling when it is part of an official name.

## Capitalisation

Use sentence case for:

- page titles
- headings
- form questions and labels
- buttons
- links
- table headings
- confirmation messages
- email subject lines

Write:

- Calculate your government pension
- Check your application
- Contact the ministry
- Submit your documents

Do not write:

- Calculate Your Government Pension
- Check Your Application
- Contact The Ministry
- Submit Your Documents

Use capitals for:

- the first word of a sentence or heading
- names of people and places
- complete official names of organisations, ministries, laws, programmes and services
- recognised abbreviations such as ID and NIS

For example:

- Ministry of Health and Wellness
- Government of Barbados
- National Insurance and Social Security Service
- Barbados National ID card

After giving the complete official name, you can use a lower-case general term if the meaning remains clear.

For example:

Contact the Ministry of Health and Wellness. The ministry will tell you what happens next.

Use lower case for general terms such as:

- government
- minister
- ministry
- department
- pension
- gratuity
- ex-gratia award
- public officer
- government service

Capitalisation in a source does not prove that something is an official name. Check the name when the distinction matters.

Do not use block capitals for emphasis. Keep capitals where they form part of a recognised abbreviation or document marking, such as ID, NIS or PAID.

## Full stops

Do not put a full stop at the end of:

- page titles
- headings
- form labels that are not questions
- buttons
- links
- email subject lines
- short bullet points that are not complete sentences

Use a full stop at the end of:

- complete sentences
- error messages written as sentences
- numbered steps
- bullet points that are complete sentences

For example:

Heading

What happens next

Button

Submit your application

Complete sentence

We will email you when we receive your application.

## Question marks

Use a question mark when asking a direct question.

For example:

- What is your date of birth?
- Do you live in Barbados?
- Have you applied before?

Do not add a question mark to a label that is not a question.

Write:

Date of birth

Do not write:

Date of birth?

## Colons

Use a colon when the words before a list lead directly into it.

For example:

You need:

Do not add a colon to a heading simply because a list appears below it.

## Semicolons

Avoid semicolons. They can make sentences harder to read.

Use a full stop or a list instead.

## Dashes and hyphens

Do not use em dashes or en dashes in public-facing content.

Do not replace an em dash with a spaced hyphen. Rewrite the sentence using a full stop, a comma or a shorter sentence.

Use a hyphen when it is part of a recognised word or helps prevent confusion.

For example:

- self-employed worker
- ex-gratia award

Do not use a dash or hyphen for a date, time or number range. Use “to”.

## Ampersands and slashes

Write “and” instead of using an ampersand, unless the ampersand is part of an official name.

Write “or” instead of using a slash to show alternatives.

Write:

Passport or Barbados National ID card

Do not write:

Passport/ID card

## Exclamation marks and ellipses

Do not use exclamation marks in routine government service content.

Avoid ellipses in final public content. Complete the sentence or leave out the unfinished wording.

## Brackets

Use round brackets sparingly. They can be useful for short supporting information, but important information should appear in the main sentence.

Do not use brackets to combine singular and plural wording.

Write:

Upload the documents you have.

Do not write:

Upload the document(s).

## Apostrophes and commas

Use apostrophes correctly in names and possessive words.

Do not use an Oxford comma unless leaving it out could make the meaning unclear.

Do not automatically add a comma because a sentence contains “and” or “but”. Add one only when it helps the sentence make sense.

## Quotation marks

Use single quotation marks when quoting a word or short phrase.

Use double quotation marks for a quotation inside another quotation.

Do not use quotation marks for emphasis.

## Lists

Keep the grammar and punctuation consistent within each list.

For bulleted lists:

- use at least 2 bullet points
- include a lead-in when the bullet points depend on it
- make sure each bullet reads naturally from the lead-in
- begin each bullet in the same grammatical form
- do not add “and” or “or” after individual bullet points
- do not end bullet points with semicolons
- do not mix short phrases and complete sentences in the same list

Do not use full stops when all the bullet points are short phrases.

For example:

You need:

- proof of address
- a valid form of identification
- your reference number

Use full stops when every bullet point is a complete sentence.

For example:

Before you apply:

- Check that your identification has not expired.
- Ask your referee for permission to include their details.
- Make digital copies of the documents you need.

## Numbered steps

Use numbered steps only when the order matters or the user must follow a sequence.

Write each step as a complete sentence and end it with a full stop.

Start a new numbered list at 1.

## Dates in written content

When a date appears in a sentence or other written content, write the month first and include the ordinal ending.

Use:

- May 5th, 2026
- Applications close on May 5th, 2026.
- You must submit the documents by May 5th, 2026.
- Last updated May 5th, 2026

Do not use:

- 5 May 2026
- May 5, 2026
- 05/05/2026
- 05/05/26

Include the year when users could misunderstand which year is meant. You can leave it out when the surrounding content makes the year completely clear.

Use “to” for date ranges.

For example:

- May 5th to 10th, 2026
- November 10th to December 21st, 2026

Use an exact date instead of a relative term when content may remain online.

Write:

Applications close on September 6th, 2026.

Avoid:

Applications close next Friday.

Use “on or before” when a deadline includes the date given and that meaning has been confirmed.

For example:

Apply on or before September 6th, 2026.

This format applies to dates written by GovTech and dates displayed back to users as part of a service.

It does not require us to change:

- a date entered by a user
- an exact quotation
- prescribed legal wording
- the title of a source document

## Dates entered into a system

Dates entered into a form follow the Ask for a date pattern, not the written-content date format.

For example:

27 03 2012

The Ask for a date pattern covers the fields, hint text, validation and error messages.

## Times

Write times without full stops or unnecessary zeros.

Use:

- 9am
- 5:30pm
- 9am to 5pm
- midday
- midnight

Do not use:

- 9:00 a.m.
- 0900hrs
- 9am-5pm
- 12 noon

Use “to”, not a dash or hyphen, for a time range.

Use 11:59pm instead of midnight when users could misunderstand whether the deadline falls at the beginning or end of a date.

## Numbers

Use numerals for:

- fees
- ages
- deadlines
- quantities
- percentages
- time estimates

Keep the unit beside the number.

Use “to” for a number range.

For example:

5 to 10 working days

Make age limits and other boundaries clear.

Write:

aged 16 or older

Avoid:

16+

## Money

Use the same Barbados currency format throughout a service.

Use:

- BDS $100
- BDS $60,000

Do not mix “BDS $”, “BBD” and dollar-only formats within the same service unless it genuinely uses more than one currency.

## Percentages

Use the percentage symbol with a numeral.

Write:

10%

Do not write:

10 percent

## Abbreviations and official names

Do not use full stops in abbreviations or titles.

Use:

- Dr
- Mr
- ID
- NIS

Do not use:

- Dr.
- Mr.
- I.D.
- N.I.S.

Write an unfamiliar abbreviation in full the first time it appears, followed by the abbreviation in brackets.

You can use the abbreviation by itself after that if it helps the user.

Do not expand an abbreviation when it is better known than the full term and there is evidence that users understand it.

Use the complete official name of a ministry, department, agency, law, programme or service when its identity matters. You can use a lower-case general reference afterwards if the meaning remains clear.

## Check the content

Before publishing or handing over the content, check:

- Is UK English used throughout?
- Are titles, headings, labels, buttons and links in sentence case?
- Are official names capitalised correctly?
- Are general references in lower case?
- Are full stops and question marks used consistently?
- Do all lists use consistent grammar and punctuation?
- Have semicolons, em dashes and en dashes been removed?
- Are alternatives written using words instead of slashes?
- Do written dates use the agreed format?
- Do date input fields follow the Ask for a date pattern?
- Are times, number ranges, money and percentages formatted consistently?
- Are abbreviations written without full stops?
- Has exact legal or prescribed wording been preserved?

## When to ask for help

Check with the content owner if an existing approved standard conflicts with this guide.

Speak to the appropriate service, policy or legal owner before making a change that could affect:

- legal meaning
- a prescribed statement
- an official name
- eligibility
- an obligation
- a deadline
- another important service rule

You do not need to escalate straightforward corrections that do not change the meaning.
